Notes

1. Remember to set the zero and span offset values to zero on the machine before starting the calibration.

2. Zero offset is the baseline correction at the lower calibration point; it shifts the temperature reading up or down by a fixed amount.

3. Span offset is the gain correction across the range; it adjusts the slope so high-temperature readings track the reference correctly.

4. Max deviation is the worst overall mismatch from the fitted calibration line across all points; lower values mean better probe accuracy.

Enter any one power value. The others follow the voltage, supply, and power factor.

Line current

—

Notes

1. Single phase: kW = V × I × PF / 1000. Current is I = (kW × 1000) / (V × PF).

2. Three phase: kW = √3 × V × I × PF / 1000. V is the line-to-line voltage, and I is the line current.

3. DC: kW = V × I / 1000. Power factor is not used, so kVA matches kW and kVAR is zero.

4. kVA = kW / PF. kVAR = √(kVA² − kW²), shown as a magnitude for a lagging power factor.

5. Use a power factor of 1.0 for heaters and other resistive loads. Motors are often about 0.8 to 0.9.

6. Horsepower is the electrical equivalent of the real power, using 745.7 W per hp. BTU/h is the heat equivalent, using 3412.14 BTU/h per kW.
